Method

The methodological framework for this study is designed to measure temporal behavior in entropy without depending on encoding, resolution, device, or manifold. To accomplish this, a resolution invariant and manifold independent computational pipeline was developed and applied to a wide range of entropy sources. These sources include hardware entropy from multiple machines, atmospheric entropy from Random.org, tunneling entropy, symbolic encodings, inverted encodings, PRNG drift captured through the Temporal Thermometer 2.0 Android probe, and raw temporal dynamics that were analyzed without clustering. Each dataset represents a distinct modality and was processed independently before being compared across manifolds to evaluate temporal geometry invariance.

Tunneling entropy was collected using a custom LED electron tunneling noise generator designed specifically for this study. The circuit consists of a dual LM358P operational amplifier powered by a 5 V USB input. A 4.7 kΩ bias resistor and a 0.1 µF coupling capacitor form the tunneling junction across a clear epoxy LED, producing stochastic electron tunneling events that generate a broadband noise signal. This signal is amplified through the LM358P and coupled via a 0.1 µF output capacitor to a 3.5 mm stereo audio interface connected to a Sabrent USB sound card. The probe was mounted on a foam block with female‑female jumper wires and DIP‑8 breadboard adapters to ensure mechanical stability and electrical isolation. The resulting analog signal was digitized at 48 kHz and recorded as raw entropy data for subsequent temporal‑geometry analysis. This device served as the primary hardware entropy source for the tunneling datasets used in this study.

Temporal geometry is revealed by mapping entropy into structured manifolds. Five manifold types were used in this study. The hexagram manifold provides a six‑bit symbolic structure. The byte manifold provides an eight‑bit numeric structure. The sixteen‑bit and sixty‑four‑bit manifolds extend this numeric representation to higher resolutions. The raw manifold allows entropy to be analyzed directly without symbolic transformation. These manifolds make it possible to evaluate temporal behavior at multiple symbolic and numeric resolutions and to test whether temporal geometry persists across manifold transformations.

All datasets undergo the same preprocessing steps. Entropy values are normalized to a uniform range. Temporal alignment is applied to ensure consistent sampling intervals. Noise filtering is used only for hardware datasets to remove device‑specific artifacts. Raw entropy is then encoded into the chosen manifold. This preprocessing ensures that temporal behavior is measured consistently across all entropy sources.

To evaluate temporal geometry at multiple resolutions, each dataset is clustered into a fixed number of states. The values of k used in this study are eight, sixteen, thirty‑two, sixty‑four, and one hundred twenty‑eight. Clustering is performed using uniform partitioning rather than k‑means to preserve temporal continuity. Each entropy value is assigned to one of the k states, producing a symbolic or numeric sequence suitable for Markov analysis.

For each manifold and resolution, a state transition matrix is constructed. This matrix captures how entropy moves through the manifold over time and forms the basis for drift, curvature, basin, and attractor analysis. The stationary distribution is computed from the transition matrix and reveals long‑term equilibrium behavior. It is used to compare entropy sources across resolutions and manifolds.

Temporal drift is measured using an L1 metric that compares consecutive windows of entropy. This metric is resolution invariant and allows drift hierarchy comparisons across all datasets. Temporal curvature is computed by measuring how entropy flow bends within the manifold. Symbolic encodings amplify curvature, atmospheric entropy produces zero curvature, and inverted encodings produce symmetry‑reflected curvature.

Basins are identified by locating states with high incoming probability and low outgoing probability. Attractors are states with high self‑transition probability. Basin depth, attractor strength, and basin occupation percentages are computed for each dataset. Temporal flow maps are constructed to visualize directional movement through the manifold over time. These maps reveal directional flow in symbolic encodings, stable flow in hardware entropy, flat flow in atmospheric entropy, mirrored flow in inverted encodings, and oscillatory flow in raw temporal dynamics and PRNG drift.

All metrics are computed at every resolution. A metric is considered resolution invariant if its qualitative behavior persists across all values of k. Entropy sources are also analyzed across all manifolds. Temporal geometry is considered manifold independent if oscillation persists, drift hierarchy remains stable, curvature signatures remain consistent, and basin structures remain reproducible across hexagram, byte, sixteen‑bit, sixty‑four‑bit, and raw manifolds.

This methodological framework enables the detection of temporal geometry across multiple entropy sources, multiple devices, multiple manifolds, multiple resolutions, symbolic and numeric encodings, and raw temporal dynamics. It is the first pipeline capable of demonstrating universal temporal oscillation and encoding‑independent temporal structure.
