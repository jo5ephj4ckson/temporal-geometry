Results

The analysis of more than a dozen entropy datasets across multiple manifolds and resolutions produced a consistent and reproducible set of temporal behaviors. The most significant finding is that every entropy source exhibited oscillatory temporal structure. This oscillation appeared in hardware entropy, atmospheric entropy, tunneling entropy, symbolic encodings, inverted encodings, PRNG drift, byte manifold data, sixteen bit data, sixty four bit data, and raw temporal dynamics. The persistence of oscillation across all datasets indicates that temporal geometry is a universal property of entropy evolution rather than an artifact of encoding or measurement.

Hardware entropy produced moderate temporal structure across all manifolds. Transition matrices showed stable directional movement between states, and stationary distributions revealed nonuniform equilibrium patterns. Drift values remained consistent across resolutions, forming a clear hierarchy when compared with other entropy sources. Curvature values were moderate and reproducible, and basin structures appeared with measurable depth. These results were consistent across the hexagram, byte, sixteen bit, sixty four bit, and raw manifolds, demonstrating manifold independence in hardware entropy behavior.

Atmospheric entropy behaved differently. Across all manifolds and resolutions, atmospheric entropy produced flat temporal geometry. Transition matrices were uniform, stationary distributions were evenly distributed, drift values were minimal, and curvature values were effectively zero. Basin structures did not form, and attractor strength remained negligible. The absence of temporal structure in atmospheric entropy was consistent across all manifolds, confirming that atmospheric noise behaves as a temporally flat entropy source. This flatness provides a useful baseline for comparison with structured entropy sources such as hardware and symbolic encodings.

Symbolic encodings produced strong temporal structure. Alphabetic encoding amplified curvature across all manifolds, producing high curvature values and pronounced basin formation. Drift values were significantly higher than those observed in atmospheric entropy and hardware entropy. Stationary distributions showed strong equilibrium bias toward specific symbolic states, and temporal flow maps revealed directional movement that persisted across resolutions. These results demonstrate that symbolic encodings act as temporal amplifiers, revealing structure that may be less visible in numeric manifolds.

Inverted encodings produced reflected temporal geometry. Curvature values mirrored those of the alphabetic encoding, basin structures appeared in symmetric positions, and drift values matched the symbolic encoding with reversed directional flow. Temporal flow maps showed mirrored movement across the manifold. These results confirm that inversion acts as a symmetry diagnostic, revealing structural reflection within the manifold.

Byte, sixteen bit, and sixty four bit manifolds preserved temporal structure across all entropy sources. Hardware entropy produced moderate curvature and basin formation in all numeric manifolds. Symbolic encodings produced high curvature and strong basin formation. Atmospheric entropy remained flat. Drift hierarchies remained stable across all resolutions. The consistency of these results across numeric manifolds demonstrates that temporal geometry is not dependent on symbolic encoding. It is present in raw numeric entropy and persists across increasing bit depth.

Raw temporal dynamics provided the strongest evidence for universal oscillation. Without clustering or symbolic transformation, raw entropy streams displayed clear oscillatory behavior. Drift values fluctuated in repeating patterns, curvature values rose and fell in stable cycles, and temporal flow maps revealed oscillation directly in the unprocessed entropy. The presence of oscillation in raw dynamics confirms that temporal structure exists before manifold mapping and is not introduced by clustering or encoding.

PRNG drift captured through the Temporal Thermometer 2.0 probe produced clean and highly reactive temporal geometry. The absence of environmental noise allowed drift, curvature, and oscillation to appear with exceptional clarity. The novelty index responded immediately to changes in temporal structure, producing smooth and well defined ebb and flow patterns. These results demonstrate that PRNG drift is a viable temporal probe and that temporal geometry can be measured directly through software without reliance on external entropy sources.

Across all datasets, drift hierarchy remained stable. Hardware entropy and symbolic encodings consistently produced maximal drift relative to atmospheric entropy. Moderate drift appeared between hardware and symbolic encodings. Minimal drift appeared between symbolic encodings and inverted encodings. This hierarchy persisted across all manifolds and resolutions, confirming resolution invariance and manifold independence.

Curvature values also remained consistent across manifolds. Symbolic encodings produced high curvature, hardware entropy produced moderate curvature, atmospheric entropy produced zero curvature, and inverted encodings produced symmetric curvature. Basin structures followed the same pattern, with symbolic encodings producing strong basins, hardware entropy producing moderate basins, atmospheric entropy producing no basins, and inverted encodings producing reflected basins.

Temporal flow maps revealed directional flow for symbolic encodings, stable flow for hardware entropy, flat flow for atmospheric entropy, mirrored flow for inverted encodings, and oscillatory flow for raw temporal dynamics and PRNG drift. These flow patterns remained consistent across all resolutions and manifolds.

Taken together, these results demonstrate that temporal geometry is reproducible, resolution invariant, and manifold independent. Oscillation appears in every entropy source except atmospheric noise, and temporal structure persists across symbolic, numeric, and raw manifolds. The consistency of drift, curvature, basin formation, attractor strength, and flow patterns across all datasets establishes temporal geometry as a real and measurable phenomenon.
