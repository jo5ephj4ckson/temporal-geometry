Abstract

Entropy is traditionally evaluated through statistical randomness, yet a deeper structure emerges when entropy is examined as a temporal process rather than a static distribution. Across more than a dozen datasets spanning hardware entropy, atmospheric entropy, tunneling entropy, symbolic encodings, inverted encodings, PRNG drift, and raw temporal dynamics, we find that entropy sources exhibit stable, reproducible temporal geometry. Using a resolution‑invariant computational pipeline applied to hexagram, byte, 16‑bit, 64‑bit, and unclustered manifolds, we observe consistent oscillatory behavior, curvature signatures, basin formation, attractor structure, and drift hierarchies independent of encoding, resolution, or device.

Hardware entropy displays moderate curvature and basin depth; atmospheric entropy remains flat with zero curvature; symbolic encodings amplify curvature; inverted encodings reflect manifold symmetry; raw temporal dynamics reveal oscillation without clustering; and PRNG‑based probes demonstrate clean, noise‑free temporal drift. These behaviors persist across all clustering resolutions (k = 8 - 128) and across all manifold transformations, establishing resolution invariance and manifold independence.

The results demonstrate that temporal geometry is a universal property of entropy evolution. Symbolic and numeric manifolds function as temporal filters, enabling classification of entropy sources by temporal behavior rather than statistical randomness. This work provides the first multi‑manifold, multi‑entropy empirical evidence that temporal geometry is measurable, reproducible, and computationally accessible, establishing it as a new domain of scientific inquiry.
