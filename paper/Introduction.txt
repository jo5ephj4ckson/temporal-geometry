Introduction

Entropy is usually treated as a static property measured through distributions, randomness tests, or statistical uniformity. But entropy is not static. It unfolds. It moves. It evolves over time. And when entropy is examined as a temporal process rather than a purely statistical one, a deeper structure emerges: patterns of drift, curvature, oscillation, basin formation, and attractor behavior that remain invisible to conventional randomness analysis. This work introduces and formalizes temporal geometry, the study of how entropy behaves as a dynamical system.

The foundation of temporal geometry is the observation that entropy sources produce distinct, reproducible temporal fingerprints when mapped into symbolic or numeric manifolds. Early experiments using the hexagram manifold revealed measurable curvature, basin depth, and drift hierarchy across hardware, atmospheric, symbolic, and inverted symbolic entropy sources. These findings suggested that symbolic manifolds act as temporal filters or structures that reveal the underlying temporal behavior of entropy rather than its statistical randomness.

Over the course of extended experimentation, the scope of this work expanded dramatically. New manifolds were introduced: byte, 16‑bit, 64‑bit, and raw temporal dynamics without clustering. New entropy sources were added: multiple hardware devices, atmospheric RNG, tunneling entropy, symbolic encodings, inverted encodings, PRNG drift, and high‑resolution Android entropy probes. Across more than a dozen datasets, one phenomenon appeared universally: temporal oscillation. Regardless of device, encoding, resolution, or manifold, entropy exhibited stable, repeating patterns of temporal drift.

While traditional time-series analysis applies tools like Takens' delay-coordinate embedding or Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA) to detect determinism in chaotic systems, these methods are typically deployed to study single, continuous physical or biological attractors rather than comparative entropy evolution. Conversely, classical information theory evaluates entropy through static probability distributions (Shannon) or algorithmic complexity (Kolmogorov), treating temporal fluctuations as noise to be mitigated. This work bridges that methodological divide by introducing a multi-manifold temporal geometry framework. By examining how diverse stochastic streams ranging from quantum-adjacent LED electron tunneling to hardware PRNG drift project into structured symbolic and numeric manifolds, we demonstrate that temporal dynamics exhibit universal, resolution-invariant invariants that transcend individual source statistics.

This paper consolidates the full body of temporal‑geometry research into a single scientific report. It presents the computational pipeline used to analyze entropy sources across multiple manifolds and resolutions; demonstrates resolution invariance and manifold independence; establishes curvature, drift, and basin metrics as reliable temporal descriptors; and provides empirical evidence that temporal geometry is a real, measurable, reproducible domain.

The central thesis of this work is that entropy contains temporal structure, and that symbolic and numeric manifolds reveal that structure. Temporal geometry enables classification of entropy sources by their temporal behavior rather than their statistical randomness. The discovery of universal oscillation across all datasets suggests that temporal geometry is not an artifact of encoding or measurement but rather a fundamental property of entropy evolution.
